**STUB.** Material and verified arithmetic are in
`docs/topics-in-timekeeping.md` (Antennas → *Your feed line is probably wrong*).

The claim the page exists to make: feedline compensation is likely the largest
single source of timing bias in typical datacenter deployments — which makes
the natural reading order of this topic counter-intuitive, and that is worth
saying out loud. People agonise over a survey wrong by tens of centimetres
while a cable quietly costs them tens of nanoseconds.

Two independent errors that compound, and the fixes differ:

1. **Wrong length.** The markings are usually printed on the jacket. The
   failure is not that the information is unavailable — it is that nobody walks
   the cable.
2. **Wrong velocity factor.** Worse, because it is silent. Nothing about the
   install tells you the assumption was wrong.

Worked numbers, verified — ⚠️ still check against real datasheets before this
publishes. The algebra is just *length ÷ (VF × c)*:

| | VF 0.66 (solid PE) | VF 0.85 (foam) |
|---|---|---|
| Delay down a 30 m run | ≈ 152 ns | ≈ 118 ns |

So ≈ 34 ns from one wrong assumption about a cable, on a run length a
datacenter would think unremarkable. A length error is gentler but not gentle:
at VF 0.8, every metre costs ≈ 4.2 ns.

Ends on the same conclusion as
[Don't let the receiver survey itself](/timekeeping/antennas/receiver-self-survey/):
measure it and configure it. Do not accept a default and do not guess.
