Planned spine, in order:

1. **The question people arrive with.** *Can I just average the position for a
   couple of days?* Answer it in three sentences with no arithmetic: averaging
   removes random error, not bias. Half your fixes 30 cm too far east and half
   too far west average out. All of them 1 m too far north never does.
2. **The conversion, which is the page's spine.** Light moves about 30 cm per
   nanosecond, so a position error and a time error are the same error in
   different units. 30 cm *is* 1.00 ns — that is why NIST's goal is 30 cm, and
   their 3.3 ns/m is the same constant on its side.
3. **A decision rule, not a warning.** Metres and ±15 ns: averaging is fine.
   Centimetres and sub-nanosecond: bias defeats you. Note the two halves agree —
   15 ns of vertical error is 4.5 m.
4. **The diagnostic.** Restart the survey every 24 h for 14 days. Converging on
   one answer means noise; fourteen different answers means bias. No reference
   receiver, no PPP service — it works with what you already own.
5. **The evidence.** NIST measured this on this receiver and published it, and
   could not explain the bias either.
